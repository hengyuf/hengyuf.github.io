Where the gain of a copy head comes from

A study of one trained language-model family at four sizes: when a "copy head" at the output fires, whether it replaces the attention heads that normally do the copying ("induction heads"), and why its gain gets smaller as the model grows.

AnswerWhat the copy head isQ1 When it firesQ2 Induction headsQ3 The gainNot established, limitsMethods

The answer in five sentences

  1. When it fires. The copy head puts 85 - 89 % of its probability mass on tokens that already occurred earlier in the document, most of all when the pair "current token, next token" was seen before (mean copy mass 0.54 - 0.63), inside repeated passages (0.75 - 0.97) and in code; 46 - 58 % of all copy mass sits on tokens that a model without the head already predicts with a probability of at least one half. MEASURED
  2. Is it an induction head? By where it looks, yes: about three quarters of the pointer mass that lands on the right token lands on "the token that followed an earlier occurrence of the current token", which is what an induction head does, and on exact repeats of random token sequences the model with the head is at 0.06 - 0.95 nats where the baseline is at 1.8 - 5.6. MEASURED
  3. Does it replace the trunk's induction heads? At the output yes, in the attention layers only partly: removing induction attention hurts the copy-head model 3 - 5 times less than a baseline (ratio 0.22 - 0.33), and with the head switched off its own vocabulary prediction of repeated tokens is 0.61 - 0.97 nats worse than a baseline's, but the induction attention below the last block falls by only 14 - 18 %, and the verdict of the rule fixed before the measurements is "mixed" at all four sizes. MEASURED READING
  4. Where the gain comes from. More than nine tenths of the gain (91 - 94 % at d512 - d1024, after a selection effect is removed) is on repeated tokens that the baseline gives less than 10 % probability (repeats that no seen pair announces, rare tokens, names, non-English text), and it is the small difference of two large numbers: per repeated token the copy path gives back 0.70 - 1.24 nats while the trunk's own prediction has become 0.61 - 0.97 nats worse. MEASURED
  5. Why it shrinks with size. The gain falls from 0.145 (smallest) to 0.047 (largest model; baseline loss 3.561 and 2.866) because the give-back shrinks faster than the trunk's deficit; about half of the fall is the bigger baseline predicting repeated tokens better by itself, the rest is the head removing less of what is left, and every statement rests on one training run per configuration. DERIVED READING

What the copy head is

The models are transformer language models with mixture-of-experts feed-forward layers (several small feed-forward networks per layer, a router picks which ones a token uses). They read 8,192 tokens at a time. Most blocks see only the last 2,048 tokens ("short blocks"); a few "long blocks" see everything. The trunk is the whole model below the output layer. The four model sizes are named by the width of the hidden state: d512, d768, d1024 and d1280 (512 to 1,280 numbers per token).

The copy head (short tag cph) adds a second way to produce the next token. Besides the usual scores for every word of the vocabulary, the output layer also scores every earlier position of the document ("slot"). A slot stands for the token that sits at that position. The slot scores are a dot product between a query made from the final hidden state and the keys that the last block already computed for its own attention. All scores, vocabulary and slots, go through one softmax (the function that turns scores into probabilities).

s(i) = g · (q · k_i) / √h + b score of slot i (i at or before the current position, same document) C = log Σ_i exp s(i) all slots lse = log Σ_v exp z(v) all words m = sigmoid(C − lse) copy mass: the share of the output that goes through the slots p(y) = (1 − m) · p_vocab(y) + m · a(y) a(y) = share of the slot weights on slots that hold token y

q = a learned projection of the final hidden state; k_i = the last block's key at position i; g (gain) and b (bias) are learned scalars; h = the key width. The slot weights (softmax of s over the slots) are called the pointer on this page. Nothing else in the model changes: with the bias set to minus infinity the head is exactly off and the model's own vocabulary softmax remains ("head off").

Words used on this page

  • Target: one prediction of the next token. Recurrence: the target token already occurred in the document (57.5 % of all targets); first occurrence: it did not (41.8 %).
  • Pair seen: an earlier occurrence of the current token was followed by the target (26.4 % of targets). This is the case a classical induction head solves. Pair new: the target occurred before, but not after the current token (31.1 %).
  • Induction slot: a position whose preceding token equals the current token.
  • On-label share: the share of the pointer on slots that hold the target.
  • Baseline / host: the same architecture trained without the head. Loss: negative log probability of the target in nats (natural-log units); lower is better. Macro loss: the mean of the 16 per-set losses of the public Paloma evaluation suite.
  • LR: learning-rate multiplier of a run (0.8, 0.9 or 1.0).

Models and official results

Three arms carry the head: cphb4 = copy head on the plain baseline (studied in most detail); arfycphb4 and arfycph = the same head on a second host architecture (tag arfy: the baseline plus two changes to how blocks are wired together, not the subject of this study), the latter starting training with the head shut. The head has one key group at d512 and d768 and two at d1024 and d1280.

Which d1280 models were available. For d1280 the study used the pair at LR 0.8 (baseline 2.86643, with the head 2.81985, gain 0.0466). The pair behind the official d1280 gain of 0.0445 (LR 0.9: 2.86526 and 2.82075) had no saved model, so it appears only through its training logs. For the arfy family at d1280 the three LR 0.9 models were used; its host run is weak (2.89833, above the plain baseline). The LR 0.8 arfy-family runs and all LR 1.0 runs at d1280 were not finished and were not used. No d1280 statement has a noise yardstick.

Q1. In what context does the copy head fire?

"Fires" is measured by the copy mass m. It has three different answers depending on what is asked: where the mass per token is highest, where most of the mass is spent, and where the head earns its gain. They are not the same places. All numbers in this section compare the copy head on the plain baseline with the baseline of the same LR, on all 59.4 million targets of the evaluation suite, unless a figure says otherwise. Everything about the copy mass and the pointer is measured inside one forward pass of one model and has no run-to-run noise; every gain is a difference between two training runs and does.

Q2. Do the attention layers change? Does the head take over what induction heads do?

An induction head is an attention head that, at a token it has seen before, looks at what followed that token last time, so that the model can repeat it. Three questions are separated here: is the copy head's pointer itself such a head; did the trunk's attention heads change; and what does the output now depend on.

The pointer is an induction head by a rule fixed in advance

Rule: "induction head" if at least half of the on-label mass is on induction slots and the on-label share is at least 1.5 times higher on pair-seen than on pair-new targets. VERIFIER The two analyses used two weightings for the same quantities. This page uses one: every target weighted as in the macro loss, then by copy mass. Averaging 16 per-set ratios instead gives 0.70 - 0.72 and 2.0 - 2.5; unweighted per target 0.60 - 0.63 and 3.0 - 3.4. The rule passes under all three at the four sizes of the plain pair; the eight arfy-arm models pass it under the two weightings of the analyses (the unweighted variant was computed for the plain pair only).

READING It is not only an induction head: 38 - 42 % of the pointer's mass on recurrences is on slots with no matching context at all, and the pair-new targets, where no induction slot can hold the answer, carry most of the gain. The gain is mostly not the induction part.

Per-head comparison: baseline against the copy-head model

Attention pattern of every head (rows = blocks, columns = heads)

How to read the maps. A score is the share of a head's attention weights on a kind of position, averaged over targets; it says where a head looks, not how much it writes (the models scale each head's output by a learned factor afterwards, which the score does not include). Head numbers are arbitrary: two separately trained models cannot be compared head by head, only by block sums and by how many strong heads exist.

Induction attention summed over the heads of each block

Sum over a block's heads of the induction score. The pointer reads the keys of the last block.

"LR spread" = largest difference among the three baselines of a size (LR 0.8 / 0.9 / 1.0); a change counts as outside noise when it exceeds twice that. d1280 has one baseline, so no yardstick. Measured on the 12.3-million-target subset.

VERIFIER The first analysis concluded "the trunk keeps its induction attention; it is moved, not removed", because the summed score of the top 10 % of heads is 0.90 - 0.98 of the baseline's. That holds only when the last block's heads are counted. Below the last block the induction attention falls by 14 - 18 % in sum and by 8 - 16 % in the top heads, outside twice the LR spread at d512 - d1024 for the top heads. The last block does gain an induction pattern (its label-aware score is 1.8 / 5.3 / 4.3 / 5.1 times the baseline's), but zeroing that whole block costs the head model's output only 0.03 - 0.05 nats on pair-seen targets. "Moved" as a working circuit is not established: the last block looks like an induction block, and the output does not use it.

On the arfy host the direction is reversed: its last block is already an induction block without any head (score 0.67 - 1.43 against 0.17 - 0.32 in the plain baseline), and with the head it loses part of that. MEASURED

The switched-off test

The head is switched off in the trained copy-head model (same forward pass, the model's own vocabulary softmax alone) and compared with a baseline that never had a head.

Loss per token: baseline, copy-head model, copy-head model with the head off

Plain pair, all 59.4 million targets. d1280 is the LR 0.8 pair.

U = head off minus baseline (how much worse the model's own vocabulary prediction is than a baseline's); B = head off minus full model (what the copy path gives back); gain = B - U; take-over ratio = U / B. Two baselines of one size differ by at most 0.0025 per recurrence and 0.0104 per first occurrence, so U and B are far outside noise.

The ablation tests

Knock-out: induction positions removed from the attention of every head in every block

250 rows of the evaluation suite; pair-seen targets unless a column says otherwise. "Damage" = loss with the knock-out minus the control loss in the same row. "Random" = the same number of random other positions removed. The copy head's own pointer is left untouched by this test: it is itself an induction mechanism that the knock-out does not remove.

  • Ratio of the damage, copy-head model / baseline: 0.300 / 0.325 / 0.220 / 0.285. Between two baselines of one size the same ratio is 0.95 - 0.99, so this is far outside run-to-run noise. The output of the copy-head model depends on induction attention 3 - 5 times less. DERIVED VERIFIER confirmed
  • The head model's own trunk (head off) still depends on it: damage 1.19 / 1.29 / 1.31 / 1.36 nats, 51 - 69 % of the baseline's, from a control loss that is already 0.8 - 1.5 nats worse. MEASURED
  • Under the knock-out the pointer keeps most of its aim: its mean on-label share on recurrences falls by 25 / 24 / 17 / 20 % (random knock-out: below 1 %). MEASURED

Zeroing each model's own strongest induction heads

The output of the top 10 % of heads by induction score (3 / 5 / 9 / 13 heads) is set to zero; damage on pair-seen targets in nats; "floor" = mean damage of three random head sets of the same size in the same blocks.

VERIFIER The ratio from this test (0.19 - 0.40 in the first analysis) is not a clean number; the knock-out ratio above is the robust one.
  • The head model's top set contains 1 of 3 / 2 of 5 / 2 of 9 / 3 of 13 last-block heads, which its output does not use; the baseline's contains none. The head model therefore loses fewer working heads.
  • The ratio depends on how many heads are zeroed. Raw damage ratio at d1280: 0.75 with 4 heads, 0.81 with 8, 0.40 with 13; at d768 0.55 (4) and 0.33 (8); at d1024 0.42 (4) and 0.31 (8).
  • At d1024 one of the three random head sets costs the head model 0.33 nats (the others 0.05 - 0.06), so the ratio there is 0.08 - 0.26 depending on the random set.
  • How much the pointer's aim depends on these heads is not specific at d1024: zeroing them lowers its on-label share by 17 %, one random head set of the same size lowers it by 19 %. At the other sizes random sets move it by 6 % or less against 24 - 26 %.

Two more passes. Zeroing all heads of the last block: damage on pair-seen targets 0.03 / 0.05 / 0.04 / 0.04 for the head model's output and no change in the pointer's aim (the pointer's keys are made below that block's attention output). Zeroing the strongest "previous token" heads collapses the pointer's aim (on-label share 0.39 - 0.43 down to 0.04 - 0.10), but that pass breaks the whole model (it costs 1.6 - 4.9 nats on first occurrences and 2.4 - 5.4 on pair-seen targets), so it is not specific: "the pointer needs the previous-token heads" is not established. Repeated sequences

Synthetic documents "sequence, then the same sequence again"; loss per token on the second copy. Random token ids are far from the training data: supporting evidence only. The three d1024 baselines span 1.94 - 2.05 (random, 128) and 0.067 - 0.077 (passage, 2,040).

On random sequences the copy-head model does the pure induction task far better than the baseline ever did (0.06 - 0.95 against 1.8 - 5.6 nats), and with the head off its trunk is 5 - 7 nats worse than the baseline. On repeated natural passages of 2,040 tokens the baselines from d768 up are already near zero and the head adds nothing (d768: 0.150 against 0.161, inside the spread of the three baselines, 0.148 - 0.169). MEASURED VERIFIER confirmed

Where in the network the deficit appears

Reading the stream after each block with the model's own output layer (a "logit lens"), head off: at d1024 after the eighth of eleven blocks (the second long block) the baseline is at 2.00 nats on pair-seen targets (baselines at other LRs: 1.80 - 2.08) and the head model's trunk at 3.46; at the output 1.02 against 2.01. The deficit is there mid-network, not only at the end. The lens is rough (at the first blocks the trunk reads better than the baseline) and cannot separate "the stream carries less" from "the output layer reads less". MEASURED READING

Verdict

Mixed, at all four sizes (rule fixed before the measurements; neither "replaced", "re-routed" nor "adds on top" fits).

VERIFIER What the tests support, by strength of evidence:

  • By intervention: with the head, the model's output survives the removal of induction attention 3 - 5 times better than a baseline; and its own vocabulary softmax is far worse on repeats than a baseline's and still depends on induction attention.
  • By timing inside one run: the vocabulary softmax falls behind when the head opens.
  • Only by comparing two runs: that the trunk's induction attention shrank (below the last block) or moved (into the last block).
  • Not tested: which heads write the pointer's keys and query.

The first analysis added "nearest to replaced" (d512 - d1024) and "nearest to re-routed" (d1280). The verifier drops this: it turns on the two unstable numbers of the head-zeroing test.

The gain is the difference of two larger numbers

Give-back, un-learning and net gain

B = what the copy path adds to the model's own vocabulary softmax; U = how much worse that softmax is than the baseline; gain = B - U. Plain pair.

Why the gain shrinks with size

Plain pair. Gain per recurrence = baseline loss per recurrence times the fraction the head removes.

  • From d512 to d1280 the gain per recurrence keeps a factor 0.32: the baseline's loss per recurrence keeps 0.76, the fraction removed 0.42. Both B and U shrink; B shrinks faster (factor 0.57 against 0.63). DERIVED
  • The recurrences the baseline gets badly wrong (probability below 0.01) are 16.8 / 13.6 / 11.5 / 10.4 % of the recurrences. In that bin the head's gain per token falls from 1.44 to 0.79 nats, its copy mass from 0.34 to 0.24 and the on-label share of that mass from 6.2 % to 3.6 %: on the tokens the bigger baseline still misses, the pointer finds the target less often. Can one "gain per kind of token" function explain all sizes?

The test: measure the head's mean gain per cell (kind of target times how well the baseline predicts it) at one size, apply it to the baseline of another size, and compare with the gain measured there.

What is not established, and the limits

Not established

  • That induction attention "moved" into the last block as a working circuit. The last block of the copy-head model looks like an induction block, but its output is hardly used (0.03 - 0.05 nats).
  • That the trunk lost the information needed to predict repeats. Only its vocabulary softmax is shown to be untrained there.
  • Which heads write the pointer's keys and query, and so whether the pointer needs the trunk's "previous token" heads. The one test of this is not specific.
  • How much the pointer depends on the trunk's induction heads at d1024 (the effect is no larger than that of a random head set), and the head-zeroing ratio at any size.
  • The cost on first occurrences at d1280 (0.008 nats per token, inside the difference between two baselines).
  • That "the baseline got better" explains most of the shrinking. About half is supported.
  • Any d1280 statement against noise, and anything about the official d1280 pair beyond its logged losses.

Limits

  • One training run per configuration. The only noise yardsticks are runs of the same arm at neighbouring LRs (macro up to 0.005; per recurrence up to 0.0025 - 0.005; per first occurrence up to 0.010) and three repeated runs (macro 0.0008 - 0.0018). They exist for d512 - d1024, not for d1280.
  • d1280. See the box at the top: the plain pair is LR 0.8, not the official LR 0.9 pair; the arfy host at d1280 is a weak run; unfinished runs were not used.
  • Mismatched LRs on the arfy host. Four of the eight arfy-family head models are compared, for attention and ablations, with a host of another LR. They are not counted in the conclusions.
  • An ablation shows what a trained model leans on, not what a model trained without those heads would do. Zeroing a head also removes whatever else it does.
  • "Head off" is the adapted trunk, not a baseline with a head bolted on: B and U are not what a head on a frozen baseline would give.
  • Binning by the baseline's loss carries a selection effect. It was measured and removed for d512 - d1024 where this page says so; not for d1280.
  • Pointer and attention statistics come from a 12.3-million-target subset (six large sets); copy mass and on-label share agree with the full set within 0.002.
  • The data lack the exact position the pointer chose (only its distance bin and whether it holds the target), per-token attention, and a per-head attribution of the output.
  • Not re-checked by the verifier: token types, frequency groups and match length; the own-trunk-by-copy-mass table; the training-course tables; the fine-cell transfer function; the arfy head-off rows.
  • Proxies. "Names" are capitalised word starts inside a sentence. "First occurrence" means first in what the model sees: about a third of the targets are in a document piece cut by the start of the input.

Methods in brief

  • Models. 26 trained models at their final step: at each of four sizes the plain baseline (three LRs at d512 - d1024, one at d1280), the copy head on it, the arfy host and the two arfy arms with the head. All were run with the exact code version they were trained with.
  • Data. The 16 sets of the public Paloma evaluation suite as the training pipeline scores them: 7,253 inputs of 8,192 tokens, 59.4 million targets. Pointer, attention and ablation statistics on a fixed subset of 1,497 inputs (12.3 million targets); the knock-out on 250 of those. All means are "macro": a target of a set counts 1 / (16 times the set's size).
  • Per-token quantities. One forward pass gives the full loss, the vocabulary-only loss (head off), the two log-sums C and lse, and so the copy mass m and the on-label share a. The identity between them holds within 6e-6 on every model.
  • Classes. Earlier occurrences and distance of the target, the longest earlier match of the context, pair seen / pair new, ten frequency groups of token ids, token type from the token's text, position, evaluation set.
  • Head scores. For every attention head the mean share of its weights on induction slots, on slots that hold the target, and on the previous token; chance = uniform weights.
  • Interventions. Head off (exact, same forward pass); zeroing chosen heads' outputs (own top heads, random sets, the last block); removing induction positions from every head's attention with a random-position control; synthetic repeated sequences; reading intermediate streams with the output layer.
  • Fixed in advance. The class definitions, the rule for calling the pointer an induction head, the verdict rule for Q2 and the 20 % line for the transfer test were written down before the numbers existed. One exception is stated: the d1280 transfer prediction was computed after the d1280 data existed.
  • Verification. A separate pass rebuilt the token classes and the headline tables from the raw per-token data with its own code, read the measurement code against its description, and attacked each statistic with pairs of baselines that have no head. Its corrections are marked on this page.
  • Examples. Windows of 64 targets selected by fixed scoring rules on the d1024 pair; each was read, and passages with spam, adult content or personal details were replaced by the next in rank.
